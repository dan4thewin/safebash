You are reviewing a Bash-to-IR compiler for semantic fidelity. The
compiler parses Bash scripts using tree-sitter-bash, then lowers the
concrete syntax tree (CST) into an intermediate representation (IR).

Your job: find Bash constructs where the IR representation LOSES
SEMANTIC INFORMATION or CHANGES BEHAVIOR compared to what Bash
actually does.

## What to Look For

1. **Lost information**: Bash constructs that are parsed but not
   represented in the IR (silently dropped)
2. **Changed semantics**: Constructs where the IR representation
   implies different behavior than actual Bash execution
3. **Ambiguous representation**: Two different Bash constructs that
   produce the same IR (conflation)
4. **Missing constructs**: Valid Bash syntax that tree-sitter-bash
   parses but the lowering code doesn't handle (falls through to
   a default case or panics)

## Key Areas to Examine

- Variable assignment: `x=1` vs `readonly x=1` vs `export x=1`
  vs `declare -i x=1` — do flags survive lowering?
- Redirections: `>` vs `>>` vs `2>&1` vs `<` vs `<<<` — are all
  redirect types represented?
- Word splitting: `$var` vs `"$var"` — does the IR preserve
  quoting information?
- Heredocs: `<<EOF` vs `<<'EOF'` vs `<<-EOF` — does the IR
  distinguish expansion vs literal?
- Process substitution: `<(cmd)` and `>(cmd)` — handled?
- Brace expansion: `{a,b,c}` and `{1..10}` — handled?
- Glob patterns: `*.txt`, `[abc]`, `?(pattern)` — handled?
- Arithmetic: `$((x+1))` vs `$(($x+1))` vs `let x=1` — handled
  consistently?
- Traps: `trap 'cmd' SIGNAL` — represented?
- Coprocesses: `coproc` — handled?

For each finding, provide:

1. **The Bash construct** (concrete script snippet)
2. **What Bash does** with it
3. **What the IR represents** (or "not represented")
4. **Impact**: What goes wrong if the compiler trusts the IR
5. **Severity**: HIGH (behavior change), MEDIUM (information loss),
   LOW (cosmetic)
